A SHARED LANGUAGE FOR DEFENDERS

Understanding MITRE ATT&CK

A public knowledge base for describing adversary behavior, built from real-world observations.

MITRE ATT&CK (Adversarial Tactics, Techniques, and Common Knowledge) helps security teams describe what an adversary may be trying to achieve and the behaviors they may use. Teams use it to organize threat intelligence, review detection coverage, and communicate consistently. It is a reference for analysis—not a step-by-step attack guide.

WHY

Tactic

The adversary's goal at a point in time, such as gaining initial access or affecting availability.

HOW

Technique

A category of behavior used to pursue a goal. A sub-technique describes a more specific variation.

OBSERVED EXAMPLE

Procedure

A particular observed use of a technique. Procedures vary; a technique label alone does not identify who acted.

HOW TO USE THE MATRIX

Go from an observation to a defensive action

  1. Choose the right matrix. Start with Enterprise for common organization IT; Mobile and ICS cover different environments.
  2. Start with evidence. Describe what was actually observed in logs, alerts, or a validated incident report.
  3. Browse by goal, then behavior. Tactic columns group adversary objectives. Open a technique or sub-technique to compare its description and examples with your evidence.
  4. Use the mapping to improve defense. Review the detection and mitigation guidance, identify coverage gaps, and record uncertainty. A match is a hypothesis to investigate—not proof of intent or attribution.

FICTIONAL DEFENSIVE WALKTHROUGH

From observations to useful questions

NOT A CONFIRMED ATTACK SEQUENCE
  1. 01

    An employee reports an unexpected email attachment.

    Possible mapping: T1566.001 — Spearphishing Attachment, under Initial Access. Verify what was delivered and what the telemetry actually shows.

  2. 02

    A successful sign-in is reported as unfamiliar by the account owner.

    Possible mapping: T1078 — Valid Accounts. Compare identity-provider logs, device context, and the user's confirmation.

  3. 03

    Several managed devices report files becoming unavailable.

    Possible mapping: T1486 — Data Encrypted for Impact. Follow the organization's incident process, preserve evidence, and coordinate safe recovery.

These are candidate labels for separate observations, not proof that the events are connected or that a specific group is responsible. ATT&CK is not a linear timeline, and a mapping should be supported by evidence.
